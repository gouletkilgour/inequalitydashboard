Why Wealth Gaps Widen Faster Than Income Gaps

Sample post · July 29, 2026

Wealth and income are often discussed together, but they behave very differently over time. Income is a flow — what a household earns in a given year — while wealth is a stock, built up through saving, investment returns, and inheritance across decades. That difference in mechanics is why wealth inequality tends to widen faster than income inequality.

Consider two households with similar incomes but different starting wealth. Over a working life, compounding returns on existing assets, access to cheaper credit, and the ability to weather downturns without selling assets all favour the household that started ahead. Small early gaps in wealth compound into large gaps decades later, even if the two households have nearly identical earnings trajectories.
