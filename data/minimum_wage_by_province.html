Current General Minimum Wage
As of September 2, 2026 — click a province to highlight it

What this shows

The general (basic adult) minimum hourly wage in each of Canada's ten provinces, plus their unweighted average, from January 1, 1974 — the date every province has a comparable general rate — through the present. The average line is shown by default; click any province, or a row in the ranking list, to highlight it instead.

Data source

Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC), "Historical Minimum Wage Rates in Canada" (dataset 390ee890-59bb-4f34-a37c-9732781ef8a0, resource wages_general-2.csv), Government of Canada Open Government Portal, downloaded September 2, 2026. This is the same underlying data behind the government's public Minimum Wage Database.

British Columbia's June 2025 and June 2026 rates were corrected against BC government news releases (2025LBR0017-000500, 2026LBR0021-000581): the source file mislabeled the $17.85 rate as effective June 1, 2026 instead of June 1, 2025.

Methodology & exclusions

  • This is the general adult minimum wage only — it excludes separate rates that applied to young/student workers or specific occupations (e.g. liquor servers, homeworkers), which are tracked separately by ESDC.
  • Sex-differentiated rates are excluded. Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, and Prince Edward Island each set explicitly lower rates for women than men for some period after 1965.
  • Regional and sectoral variation is also excluded — rather than picking one of two coexisting rates (e.g. an urban vs. a non-urban rate), each affected province's series simply starts once a single rate covered every worker in the province: Alberta (urban/non-urban split until 1966), New Brunswick (one rate for wholesale/retail/manufacturing only until 1968), Ontario (a lower Northern Ontario Zone rate until 1965), Quebec (a Montreal-area premium until 1971), Saskatchewan (a metropolitan-area premium until 1972), and Manitoba (urban/rural split until 1966).
  • Putting both exclusions together, each province's sourced record begins at the first date a single rate, with no sex, regional, or sectoral differentiation, covered the whole province: Ontario — December 27, 1965; British Columbia — January 1, 1965 (never split); Manitoba — December 1, 1966; Alberta — July 1, 1966; New Brunswick — January 1, 1968; Newfoundland and Labrador — June 1, 1972; Nova Scotia — July 1, 1972; Quebec — May 1, 1971; Saskatchewan — January 2, 1972; Prince Edward Island — January 1, 1974.
  • The chart displays every province from January 1, 1974 onward — Prince Edward Island's start date, the latest of the ten — even though most provinces' sourced record (above) goes back further, so every province is shown over the same span and is directly comparable. For a province whose record starts earlier, the displayed series' first point holds whatever rate was already in effect on January 1, 1974 (noted on hover); no earlier data is discarded, only left out of this display.
  • Data is shown only through today; rates already enacted for a future date are not included, and the page does not update automatically when new rates take effect.
  • Each province's line is extended flat to today at its last recorded rate (e.g. Alberta's line continues at $15.00 through the present) so a rate that simply hasn't changed recently still reads as in effect, rather than the line appearing to end; this doesn't add a new rate change, only a visual continuation, and is noted on hover.
  • Canada (average) is the unweighted mean of the ten provinces' rates, shown as a dashed line, red when selected (provinces turn blue when selected). It runs the same January 1, 1974 through today span as the provinces, so it's always computed across all ten.
  • Alberta and Saskatchewan currently sit at the bottom of the ranking — Alberta's general rate has not changed since October 2018.
