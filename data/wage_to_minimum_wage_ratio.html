About this data

The Ratio

Each line is a province’s hourly wage ÷ minimum wage ratio, by year. The numerator is Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey estimate of the average (mean) or median hourly wage rate, for both full- and part-time employees combined, all industries, all ages 15 and over, both sexes. The denominator is that province’s general (basic adult) minimum wage — the same series shown on the Provincial Minimum Wages page — averaged over the calendar year and weighted by how many days each rate was actually in effect, since most provinces raise their minimum wage partway through the year rather than on January 1.

Canada’s wage figures are Statistics Canada’s own national LFS estimate, not an average of the provinces. Since there is no federal minimum wage for the general workforce, Canada’s minimum-wage denominator is the unweighted mean of the ten provinces’ time-weighted annual rates — the same convention used for the Average line on the Provincial Minimum Wages page.

Mean vs. Median

The mean hourly wage is pulled upward by high earners, so mean/minimum-wage ratios run higher and are more sensitive to changes at the top of the wage distribution. The median hourly wage — the wage of the middle-ranked worker — is less affected by high earners and is generally the more meaningful benchmark for how the minimum wage compares to a “typical” worker’s pay.

A falling ratio over time means the minimum wage has risen faster than typical wages (workers near the bottom catching up); a rising ratio means typical wages have outpaced minimum-wage increases.

Sources: Statistics Canada, Table 14-10-0064-01, Employee wages by industry, annual (Labour Force Survey), downloaded 2026-09-05. Minimum wage data: Employment and Social Development Canada, Minimum Wage Database (open.canada.ca, dataset 390ee890-59bb-4f34-a37c-9732781ef8a0) — see the Provincial Minimum Wages page for full methodology notes. Both series run 1997–2025, the range for which the wage table has data.
