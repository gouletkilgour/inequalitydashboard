Wealth Shares

About this data

Wealth Shares

These series show the share of total net worth held by segments of the wealth distribution in Canada — from the top 50% down to the top 0.01%, and the bottom 20% and bottom 50%. Two sources are available from the Data source selector: Statistics Canada's Survey of Financial Security (SFS) microdata, and the Parliamentary Budget Officer's (PBO) SFS+Forbes estimates, which correct for the SFS's known under-sampling of very wealthy families — or compare both directly.

Because some families' debts exceed their assets, the bottom shares can be negative: it is possible for the least-wealthy 20% or 50% of families to hold a small negative share of aggregate net worth.

Survey of Financial Security (SFS)

The SFS is Statistics Canada's household wealth survey. It is not conducted annually; data are available for 1999, 2005, 2012, 2016, 2019, and 2023.

PBO Estimate (SFS + Forbes)

The SFS is known to under-sample very wealthy families — Statistics Canada's own SFS team has said the survey's sample size is "insufficient to have a representative sample of economic families with very high wealth." The PBO corrects for this by using Pareto interpolation (following Vermeulen, 2018) to bridge the gap between the top of the SFS and publicly available billionaire lists, then scaling the result to match Statistics Canada's National Balance Sheet Accounts total for household net worth. These figures are the PBO's own published SFS + Forbes estimates, taken directly from its reports rather than reconstructed locally.

Coverage is uneven across years and cutoffs because it follows exactly what the PBO has published: 1999–2019 only go up to Top 10%, while Top 20% is available for 2023 only. The PBO has never published this series for Top 50%, Bottom 50%, or Bottom 20%, nor has it published dollar ("Average Wealth") figures for 1999–2019.

Unit of Analysis

These series are based on the economic family unit, weighted using each cycle's survey weight so that estimates are representative of the Canadian population.

Net Worth

Net worth = Total assets (including current pensions, valued on a going-concern basis) − Total debts

Note on the top 0.01% SFS series: the SFS public use microdata file samples roughly 5,000–16,000 families per cycle, so the top 0.01% cutoff is backed by only a handful of records nationally. This series is shown as a dashed line — treat it as indicative rather than precise, especially since the PUMF also perturbs/rounds high-end values to protect respondent confidentiality.
Note on PBO uncertainty: the Pareto interpolation is sensitive to the composition of the billionaire lists and their reported net worth figures, so uncertainty at the very top of the distribution propagates through the whole series. Billionaire lists are also not perfectly consistent from year to year. The PBO's 2025 update also notes that revised methodology means its 2016/2019 figures differ slightly from those in its 2021 report.

Data sources: Statistics Canada, Survey of Financial Security Public Use Microdata Files (1999, 2005, 2012, 2016, 2019, 2023); Office of the Parliamentary Budget Officer, "Estimating the top tail of the family wealth distribution in Canada: updates and trends" (December 9, 2021), Table 3-1; and "2025 update" (September 11, 2025), Table S-1
